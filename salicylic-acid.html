THE BASICS

What is it?

Salicylic acid is a skincare ingredient that helps remove dead skin cells and unclog pores. It is a white solid that naturally occurs in willow bark but can also be made in labs. People with oily skin, blackheads, whiteheads, and acne commonly use salicylic acid.

THE SCIENCE

How does it work?

Salicylic acid acts as an exfoliant by helping loosen dead skin cells. Because it is oil-soluble, it can also get into pores and help reduce the buildup of oil and dead skin cells.

WHERE IT ACTS

What does it affect?

Salicylic acid primarily affects the skin surface and pores. It can help remove dead skin cells and reduce the buildup of sebum (oil) inside hair follicles and pores.

USES

What is it used for?

Salicylic acid is used in cosmetics and medicine. It is used as an acne treatment and can also help treat certain conditions, including psoriasis, ichthyosis, dandruff, and warts. Dermatologists may use stronger concentrations in professional chemical peels.

LOCATION

Where does it work?

Salicylic acid works on the surface of the skin and inside pores and hair follicles. It helps remove dead skin cells from the surface and reduce buildup inside pores.

04

WHO USES IT?

Who uses it?

Salicylic acid is commonly used by people with oily or acne-prone skin, clogged pores, blackheads, or whiteheads. It can also be used for certain skin conditions, such as psoriasis, dandruff, and warts.

ROUTINE

How is it used?

Salicylic acid comes in leave-on and rinse-off products, including cleansers, toners, treatments, and scalp products. Follow the instructions on the specific product.

SAFETY FIRST

What should you be careful about?

  • Do not apply it to broken or severely irritated skin.
  • Be cautious when combining it with other strong exfoliating or acne treatments.
  • Overuse can cause dryness, peeling, irritation, and damage to the skin barrier.
  • Do not apply large amounts over large areas for extended periods unless directed by a healthcare professional.

KNOW YOUR SKIN

What are the side effects?

COMMON

Mild effects

Dryness or tightness, mild redness, flaking, and temporary stinging or burning.

OVERUSE

Too much, too often

Significant dryness, peeling, burning, irritation, and skin-barrier damage.

SYSTEMIC

Uncommon

Excessive absorption can cause symptoms such as ringing in the ears, nausea, dizziness, or unusually rapid breathing. Seek medical advice if these occur after use.

Serious systemic effects are uncommon with normal topical skincare use.

STORAGE

How should it be stored?

Follow the storage instructions on the product packaging. In general, keep skincare products tightly closed and away from excessive heat and direct sunlight.

THE TIMELINE

What should you expect?

SHORT TERM

Mild dryness or light peeling may occur as your skin adjusts.

WITH CONSISTENT USE

It may help improve skin texture, keep pores clearer, and reduce blackheads and whiteheads.
