THE BASICS

What is it?

Salicylic acid is a skincare ingredient that helps remove dead skin cells and unclog pores. It is a white solid that naturally occurs in willow bark but can also be made in labs. People with oily skin, blackheads, whiteheads, and acne commonly use salicylic acid.

THE SCIENCE

How does
it work?

Salicylic acid can do many things, like breaking down dead skin, clearing clogged pores, and helping other ingredients work better.

It acts as an exfoliant by softening the outer layers of skin, which helps dead skin cells come off more easily. By removing these dead cells, it stops them from getting stuck in your pores, which reduces acne.

WHERE IT ACTS

What does
it affect?

Salicylic acid affects your skin surface and pores. It also affects dead skin cells, sebum, and hair follicles.

Skin surfacePoresSebumHair follicles

This is why it is particularly useful for clogged pores, blackheads, whiteheads, and some types of acne.

USES

What is it used for?

Salicylic acid is used in both cosmetics and medicine. It is used as an acne treatment to clear up pimples, blackheads, and pores.

It can be used to treat conditions such as psoriasis, ichthyosis, dandruff, and warts. It is also used in conditioners and acts as a preservative in cosmetics. Dermatologists use it in high concentrations in chemical peels.

LOCATION

Where does it work?

Salicylic acid works on the surface of the skin and inside pores and hair follicles.

WHO USES IT?

Who uses it?

Salicylic acid is commonly used by people with acne, specific skin conditions such as psoriasis, ichthyosis, or severe dandruff, or dermatologists who perform professional treatments.

ROUTINE

How is it used?

Salicylic acid is usually applied directly onto the skin or scalp unless the packaging instructions say otherwise. You can find leave-on products or rinse-off products depending on the condition or concern being treated.

SAFETY FIRST

What should you be careful about?

Salicylic acid is an acid, so be careful when using it. Do not apply it to damaged skin. Avoid using it with other exfoliating acids, and follow the storage instructions on your product.

  • Do not apply it to damaged or severely irritated skin.
  • Be cautious when combining it with other strong exfoliating products.
  • Using too much can cause significant irritation, dryness, peeling, and damage to the skin barrier.
  • Avoid spreading it over large areas of your body for long periods of time unless directed by a healthcare professional.

KNOW YOUR SKIN

What are the
side effects?

COMMON

Usually mild

Mild skin dryness or tightness, mild redness, peeling/flaky skin, and temporary stinging or burning.

OVERUSE

Too much, too often

Severe skin irritation, drying, redness, peeling, or burning can occur.

SYSTEMIC

Uncommon with normal use

Confusion/dizziness, headaches, fast breathing, ringing in the ears, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea can occur with excessive absorption.

Systemic and severe overuse effects are uncommon with normal topical skincare use. If you experience concerning symptoms after using a product, seek medical advice.

STORAGE

How should it
be stored?

Salicylic acid should be stored according to the instructions on the packaging. Generally, keep the product sealed and away from excessive heat and direct sunlight.

THE TIMELINE

What should
you expect?

SHORT TERM

LONG TERM
